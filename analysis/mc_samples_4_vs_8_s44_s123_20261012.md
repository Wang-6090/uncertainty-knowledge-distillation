# MC uncertainty sample-count recheck (2026-10-12)

## Purpose and controlled comparison

This detector-only stability check asks whether using more MC-Dropout samples
materially changes the uncertainty-corrected open score or its known/unknown
overlap. Four student checkpoints were fixed (CIFAR-100 semantic-isolated
60/40, seeds 44 and 123; nnPU encoder-gradient and head-only arms). Within each
checkpoint, the protocol, score mode, uncertainty weight 0.5, kNN bank,
validation-only 95%-known-coverage calibration, and test split were held fixed.
Only `--mc-samples` changed from 4 (existing run) to 8 (new run). No training
was performed.

## Conclusion

MC=8 has only tiny ranking changes relative to MC=4 (absolute AUROC changes
within 0.0004; AUPR within 0.0012), and it does not improve the operating
point consistently. FPR95 worsens in three of four arms; unknown rejection
falls in three of four arms; the overlap diagnostic is unchanged or slightly
worse in three of four arms. Thus the main finding is not an insufficient
MC sample count, and doubling MC samples does not resolve the large overlap
(roughly 71.5%-73.1% of unknown scores remain below the known q95 threshold).

The two MC runs use stochastic dropout and were not paired by saving/reusing
identical dropout masks, so small differences combine sample-count and Monte
Carlo realization effects. They should be interpreted as an operational
robustness check, not a precise causal estimate of the effect of sample count.
No default change is justified; retain MC=4 for cost unless a later paired
variance study shows a practical benefit. Next experiments should prioritize
training-time representation geometry and disjoint-validation diagnostics.
